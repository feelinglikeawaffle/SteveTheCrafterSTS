# Steve the Crafter — gameplay design

## Resource philosophy

Resource cards must repay their opportunity cost. A basic 1-energy mining card should generally generate enough material to contribute to more than one follow-up craft, rather than merely being a weak attack with a token bonus.

Use a blend:
- **Reliable mining** gives set materials and lets players plan recipes.
- **Exploration / cave mining** gives random materials and creates exciting finds.
- **Tool and Fortune effects** improve mining output or rare-resource odds.
- Avoid making every resource roll random; unlucky draws must not prevent the character from functioning.

## Starting character direction

**Character:** Steve — The Crafter

**Starting relic concept — Steve's Inventory:** At the start of combat, gain a small amount of Iron and Coal. Exact values will be tuned in playtesting.

**Starting deck concept:** mostly Wooden Sword attacks, Wooden Pickaxe resource attacks, Wooden Shield defense, and a basic Coal mining card. Exact card values are provisional.

## Card families

1. Mining — deterministic resource gain, cave exploration, ore discovery
2. Weapons — swords, axes, bows, crossbows
3. Armor / survival — shields, armor, food, healing, blocking
4. Crafting — spend materials for cards, gear, or combat effects
5. Enchantments — Sharpness, Fortune, Efficiency, Protection, Unbreaking
6. Explosives / hazards — TNT, lava, Creeper-style attacks
7. Nether / End — high-tier cards and late-run payoffs

## Crafting Table room

A special room with a custom crafting interface. It displays the player's run resources and available recipes; selecting a recipe spends materials and grants a card, relic-like run upgrade, equipment, or other reward. The room should be rare enough to feel special but appear often enough to make resource accumulation meaningful.

## Balance guardrails

- Resource generation should be strong enough to justify spending a draw and energy.
- Recipes must have clear costs and substantial effects.
- Rare materials should be exciting, but a run must not depend on finding a single rare drop.
- Keep ordinary combat functional even with a low-resource draw.
- Upgrade mining cards meaningfully (larger yield, extra material, or improved discovery odds).
- Use playtesting to finalize card counts, resource caps, room frequency, and recipe costs.
